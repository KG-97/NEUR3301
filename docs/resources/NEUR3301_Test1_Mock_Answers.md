## Rationales

### Lecture 1 — Glia I

**1. B.** One oligodendrocyte extends processes that myelinate internodes on *several different axons*. A Schwann cell forms *one* internode on *one* axon. This asymmetry matters for injury: losing one oligodendrocyte affects multiple axons at once.

**2. B.** Ependymal cells line the ventricles; their cilia contribute to CSF movement. Astrocyte end feet contact vessels, not ventricular lumen.

**3. B.** The Schwann cell repair programme is the reason peripheral nerve regenerates and CNS largely does not — dedifferentiation, myelin clearance, and formation of a permissive growth pathway. **A** describes the CNS glial scar, the opposite behaviour. **D** inverts the order: remyelination follows regrowth.

**4. B.** OPCs persist in the adult CNS and receive genuine synaptic input — one of the more surprising facts in the lecture and therefore good MCQ material. **C** is the endothelial cell. **D** is microglia.

**5. B.** The 10:1 figure is a long-standing myth. Ratios are roughly comparable overall and vary regionally. Any option asserting a uniform large ratio is wrong.

### Lecture 2 — Growth and Trophic Factors

**6. B.** Limiting supply plus competition is the whole hypothesis. **A** removes competition and makes death schedule-driven; **C** makes it autocrine, which defeats the target-matching logic the hypothesis exists to explain.

**7. B.** This is the Campenot compartmented-culture logic. Survival with NGF at terminals only demonstrates that a signal generated distally is carried retrogradely to the soma — the experimental foundation for the signalling endosome. **D** is exactly what the chamber design excludes.

**8. B.** Trk receptors are preferentially selective; p75 binds neurotrophins more broadly and can modulate or oppose Trk signalling, including pro-apoptotic outcomes. **C** is the classic overclaim — p75 does not always promote survival.

**9. B.** The signalling endosome answers "how does a signal cross a long axon intact?" — the internalised ligand–receptor complex travels retrogradely still signalling.

**10. B.** This is the neurotrophic hypothesis run in reverse. If death occurs because survival factor is *limiting*, then removing the limit should rescue the losers — and it does. **A** denies competition altogether. **D** is the key distractor: it produces the right answer by the wrong mechanism, since the effect is on *survival* of already-generated neurons, not on proliferation. Watch for distractors that get the direction right and the mechanism wrong.

*Failure type if missed: mechanism reversal, or accepting a correct prediction with a wrong causal route.*

### Lecture 3 — Axon Outgrowth, Target Recognition and Maps

**11. B.** Receptor complement and intracellular state — including cyclic nucleotide levels — determine the sign of the response. The cue does not change; the reader does.

**12. B.** Crossing changes responsiveness so the midline no longer attracts. This is the intermediate-target escape problem: reach it, then stop being attracted to it. **A** overstates — receptor changes are specific, not a wholesale permanent loss.

**13. A.** Nogo, MAG and OMgp are the myelin-associated inhibitors. **B** are neurotrophins, **C** astrocytic glutamate handling, **D** BMP antagonists — all from other lectures, which is exactly how a well-built distractor set works.

**14. B.** The gradient-versus-hurdle distinction is behavioural and measurable: graded discouragement produces slowing and steering within the field; a discrete barrier produces abrupt arrest at an edge.

**15. B.** Topographic mapping converts *relative* position into *graded* repulsion, preserving order. **A** is the discredited rigid chemoaffinity-tag reading — a unique tag per axon is not what graded systems do.

### Lecture 4 — Regressive Events and Cell Death

**16. B.** Apoptosis: regulated, energy-dependent, caspase-linked, shrinkage, relatively non-inflammatory. Necrosis: bioenergetic and osmotic failure, swelling, rupture, inflammation. **A** is the same facts swapped — read the direction, not the vocabulary.

**17. B.** BCL-2 family sets the mitochondrial checkpoint; cytochrome c release follows; apoptosome forms and caspase-9 activates. **A** reverses cause and execution — caspases execute the decision, they do not initiate it.

**18. B.** More target means more survival support, so *less* death. **A** is the trap: it sounds like competition reasoning but competition is for a limited resource, and enlarging the target enlarges the resource.

**19. B.** Whole-cell death → axon/collateral retraction → arbor and spine loss → individual synapse elimination. Regression is progressively more selective, not uniformly catastrophic.

**20. B.** TUNEL reports DNA fragmentation, which occurs in multiple death modes. It is not uniquely diagnostic of apoptosis — this is an explicit evidence-boundary point in the lecture material.

*Failure type if missed on 20: over-reading a method as pathway-specific.*

### Lecture 5 — Cell Lineage

**21. B.** Totipotent → pluripotent → multipotent → oligopotent → unipotent. Each step *restricts* options.

**22. B.** Shh ventralising, Wnt dorsalising, BMP dorsal. Straight recall, and a reversal distractor is guaranteed to appear.

**23. B.** Chick–quail chimeras exploit a nuclear marker that distinguishes donor from host, revealing origin and migratory route — a lineage method, not a physiological one.

**24. A.** The isthmus defines the midbrain–hindbrain boundary.

**25. B.** Acetylation loosens chromatin and permits transcription; deacetylation condenses it. **A** is the standard epigenetics error — access changes, sequence does not.

> **Scope note:** Reelin, reeler, Cajal–Retzius cells, VLDLR/ApoER2 and DAB1 appear nowhere in Hodgetts's Lecture 5 deck. Inside-out layering is taught as a phenomenon evidenced by label-dilution birthdating. That is why no question here requires the Reelin pathway.

### Lecture 6 — Adult Neurogenesis and Stem Cells

**26. A.** Beros's five steps: proliferation, migration, differentiation into adult identity, elaboration of dendrites and axons, and **functional synapse formation with integration**. He stressed the last one — producing a neuron is not neurogenesis unless it integrates.

**27. B.** Type 1 radial glial-like cell → Type 2 progenitor (2a less differentiated than 2b) → neuroblast → immature → mature. Some Type 1 cells become astrocytes instead.

**28. B.** Three correlated measures. Establishing that new neurons *mediate* the behavioural gain requires selectively ablating or blocking neurogenesis and showing the gain disappears. This is the single most transferable reasoning pattern in the whole unit.

*Failure type if missed: correlation-to-causation overclaim.*

**29. A.** Eriksson 1998: BrdU administered to consenting terminal cancer patients (n = 5, ~57–87 years), post-mortem hippocampus showed BrdU-positive cells, some NeuN-positive. **B** is impossible — MRI does not label dividing cells. **C** would be unethical, since tritiated thymidine is radioactive.

**30. B.** Volume is a snapshot including water, dendrites and other components. It cannot identify cell type or birth date. Contrast with BrdU, tritiated thymidine, retroviral tagging or ¹⁴C bomb-pulse dating, which time-lock division.

### Lecture 7 — Glia II

**31. B.** D-serine, released by astrocytes, is the NMDA receptor co-agonist. Other gliotransmitters named were glutamate and ATP; thrombospondins TSP1/TSP2 are separate astrocyte-derived synaptogenic factors.

**32. B.** Aquaporin-4 sits in pericapillary end-foot processes, handling water and solute exchange — a favourite pairing because the *location* is as examinable as the function.

**33. B.** Non-overlapping territories define functional domains; gap junctions couple them into a syncytial network that passes molecules below a size limit. **C** is the trap — coupled is not fused, and astrocytes remain individual cells.

**34. B.** M1 pro-inflammatory, M2 reparative (IL-10, TGF-β). Astrocytes carry the parallel A1/A2 designation. **C** swaps the naming conventions between cell types.

**35. B.** Halving the extracellular space **doubles** the concentration of anything in it. Hodgetts used this to argue that regulating extracellular space volume is itself a protective astrocyte function, not merely structural housekeeping.
