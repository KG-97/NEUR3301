### 1. L14 + L15 — compress and retrieve — 55 min
Run the playbook's four-step loop on each lecture (about 25 min each, 5 min buffer):

1. **Compress (8 min):** write the lecture's causal chain in six arrows or fewer, from the slides.
2. **Retrieve (8 min):** close everything. Redraw it from memory.
3. **Interrogate (6 min):** one blocker/lesion prediction before checking:
   - L14: *Block the mechanotransduction channel. What happens to the receptor potential, then to afferent firing, at low vs high stimulus intensity?*
   - L15: *Dorsal-column lesion vs spinothalamic lesion on one side. Which sensations are lost, and on which side?*
4. **Encode (3 min):** flashcards only for what you got wrong.

Keep the distinctions the Exam Lab already tests:
- **Receptor potential vs action potential:** graded and decremental vs all-or-none (`l14-transduction`).
- **Decussation level:** dorsal column–medial lemniscus crosses in the **medulla**; spinothalamic crosses **near the segmental level of entry** (`l15-pathways`).
- **Detection threshold vs spatial acuity:** different measurements (`l15-acuity`).

### 2. Timed long answer — 30 min, closed book
**Exam Lab → Long Answer Lab → `somatic-transduction`**

> Trace a mechanical skin stimulus from transduction to a central representation. Explain how intensity and location can be encoded, then predict the effect of a peripheral versus central lesion.

- **3 min plan:** thesis, then the causal chain.
- **24 min write.**
- **3 min repair:** check you have a thesis, a mechanism, an experiment, a limitation and a conclusion.

Use the answer grammar: **manipulation → molecular/cellular change → circuit consequence → measured outcome → alternative interpretation.**

### 3. Self-mark — 15 min

- [ ] Peripheral vs central lesion contrasted

Log each miss in the error ledger as a knowledge gap, mechanism reversal, species/context overclaim, or question-reading error.
