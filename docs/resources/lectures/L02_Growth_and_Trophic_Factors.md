## Lecturer emphasis now verified

- Developing neurons may extend initially and then acquire dependence on target-derived support after reaching the target.
- Ligand concentration is not enough to predict outcome: receptor identity, receptor co-expression, cell state, timing, location and precursor processing all matter.
- Kinesin primarily supports anterograde transport toward terminals; dynein supports retrograde transport toward the soma.
- Labelled growth-factor experiments link target uptake to axonal and somatic transport, but a visible label does not by itself prove every downstream survival mechanism.
- BDNF is regulated by activity in experimental systems and affects survival and plasticity; broad human claims about exercise, mood, sleep or cognition require separate evidence.
- Repair delivery can use direct factor application, vectors, engineered cells or scaffolds. Survival, local outgrowth, target reconnection and behavioural recovery must be measured separately.

## What you need to know cold

1. Neurotrophic factors regulate survival, differentiation, growth and plasticity. They are not merely "neuron food."
2. Preferred mature-neurotrophin pairings:
   - NGF → TrkA
   - BDNF and NT-4 → TrkB
   - NT-3 → TrkC, with context-dependent cross-activation
3. Trk receptors are receptor tyrosine kinases. Ligand binding promotes dimerisation, autophosphorylation and recruitment of signalling pathways including PI3K-Akt, Ras-MAPK and PLCγ.
4. Developing neurons can compete for limiting target-derived trophic support. Successful target access promotes survival; inadequate support can permit BIM/BAX-dependent mitochondrial apoptosis.
5. A distal ligand-receptor event can influence the soma through a retrogradely transported signalling endosome.
6. p75NTR does not have one universal output. Ligand form, sortilin-family co-receptors, Trk co-expression and cellular state can shift survival, pruning or apoptotic signalling.
7. Survival is not the same as functional repair. Correct outgrowth, target recognition, synaptogenesis and circuit function need separate evidence.

## Core causal maps

### Target-derived survival

Limited target-derived neurotrophin  
→ Trk binding and dimerisation  
→ receptor autophosphorylation  
→ PI3K-Akt / Ras-MAPK / PLCγ signalling  
→ pro-survival transcription, metabolism and differentiation

### Long-range trophic signalling

Ligand-Trk binding at distal axon  
→ endocytosis into a signalling endosome  
→ dynein-dependent retrograde transport  
→ somatic signalling and nuclear response  
→ survival/differentiation programme

### Trophic withdrawal

Insufficient trophic capture  
→ reduced Trk survival signalling  
→ pro-apoptotic BCL-2-family balance  
→ BAX-dependent mitochondrial permeabilisation  
→ cytochrome-c/caspase cascade  
→ developmental apoptosis

## High-value experiment logic

### Compartmented distal NGF experiment

- **Manipulation:** Provide NGF only to distal sympathetic axons; track internalised NGF-TrkA and interrupt retrograde transport.
- **Expected result:** Distal NGF supports somatic survival, while transport disruption weakens the nuclear survival response.
- **Inference:** A target-derived signal can remain active in an endosomal carrier during transport to the soma.
- **Limit:** Compartment cultures simplify the target environment, and dynein disruption affects other cargo.

### Developmental competition experiment

- **Manipulation:** Vary access to NGF or perturb the competition-linked sensitisation programme among developing sympathetic neurons.
- **Expected result:** Survival depends on relative trophic access, not only a fixed cell-autonomous death timer.
- **Inference:** Competition actively links target innervation to selection of surviving neurons.
- **Limit:** Sympathetic neurons are a model; do not assume every CNS population uses an identical mechanism.

### Growth-factor repair delivery

- **Manipulation:** Deliver a trophic factor directly, by vector, engineered cell or scaffold after injury.
- **Readouts:** Measure neuronal survival, axon extension, target-specific reconnection and behaviour separately.
- **Inference:** A factor can improve one repair checkpoint without completing the whole causal chain to function.
- **Limit:** Dose, spatial distribution, timing, delivery vehicle and injury state can all alter the outcome.

## Intervention predictions

| Intervention | Immediate effect | Predicted downstream outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Trk kinase inhibitor | Blocks Trk autophosphorylation | Reduced Akt/MAPK/PLCγ signalling; impaired survival or differentiation in dependent cells |
| Distal neurotrophin withdrawal | Removes target-derived input | Reduced retrograde trophic signalling; apoptosis risk rises |
| Dynein/retrograde transport disruption | Distal signal fails to reach soma efficiently | Reduced nuclear response despite local axonal ligand exposure |
| Constitutive Akt activation | Bypasses part of the Trk survival pathway | May preserve survival but does not prove correct guidance or connectivity |
| Excess exogenous BDNF | Broadens/intensifies TrkB signalling | Context-dependent growth/plasticity; may be mistimed or maladaptive |

## Examiner traps

- "Retrograde" here means transport toward the soma, not retrograde synaptic transmission.
- A trophic factor can alter survival, growth, differentiation or synaptic plasticity. The ligand name does not determine one phenotype.
- Cell counts cannot establish target recognition, synaptic integration or behavioural recovery.
- "p75NTR = death receptor" is too crude. Always state the ligand and co-receptor context.
- A correlation between neurotrophin expression and survival is weaker than a selective perturbation plus rescue.

## Long-answer skeleton

**Prompt:** Explain how target-derived trophic factors regulate developmental neuronal survival. Predict the effects of disrupting retrograde transport.

1. Thesis: target access is converted into a survival decision by ligand-specific Trk signalling communicated from distal axon to soma.
2. Define limiting target-derived support and competition.
3. Explain Trk activation and downstream survival pathways.
4. Explain signalling-endosome formation and dynein-dependent transport.
5. Predict reduced nuclear signalling, apoptosis and innervation deficits after transport disruption.
6. Give a compartment-culture experiment, expected result and rescue.
7. Limit the claim: transport affects many cargos, and survival does not prove correct connectivity.
