## Core answer

Glia are not passive “brain glue.” Their lineage, morphology, molecular state and location allow them to build, maintain, modulate, defend and sometimes restrict the nervous system.

## Lineage map

| Population | Developmental origin | Exam-safe interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Neurons, astrocytes, oligodendrocytes and ependymal cells | Neuroectoderm / neural tube | CNS macroglia do not share microglial ontogeny |
| Schwann cells and satellite glia | Neural crest | PNS glia; do not generalise this origin to CNS glia |
| Microglia | Embryonic yolk-sac erythromyeloid lineage | Resident CNS immune cells largely self-renew locally; healthy adult microglia are not normally replenished from bone marrow |

## Cell types to know cold

### Radial glia

- span the ventricular-to-pial axis during development;
- act as progenitors as well as scaffolds for migration;
- help translate developmental position and timing into cortical organisation.

Disrupt the scaffold and the predicted phenotype is mis-migration or abnormal positioning—not merely loss of generic “support.”

### Astrocytes

- buffer extracellular ions and neurotransmitters;
- support metabolism, synaptic regulation and neurovascular function;
- regulate blood–brain-barrier development and maintenance;
- change state after injury and contribute to gliosis and lesion borders.

Brain endothelial tight junctions form the principal physical blood–brain-barrier seal. Astrocytic end-feet and pericytes help maintain the specialised barrier phenotype but are not themselves the sealed endothelial wall.

### Oligodendrocytes

- form CNS myelin and support axonal function;
- one oligodendrocyte can form internodes on multiple axons.

### Microglia

- survey the CNS with motile processes;
- signal through cytokines and other mediators;
- phagocytose cellular and synaptic material;
- participate in injury responses and circuit remodelling.

M1/M2 labels are historical shorthand. In-vivo microglial states vary across region, time, disease and experimental method.

### Ependymal cells

- line the ventricular system;
- use cilia and microvilli in cerebrospinal-fluid movement and homeostasis;
- were given lower assessment emphasis than the major glial classes in this lecture.

### Olfactory ensheathing glia

- support continuing growth of olfactory axons across the PNS–CNS interface;
- have been investigated as repair cells because they can support permissive glial pathways.

### Schwann cells

- arise from neural crest and myelinate the PNS;
- one myelinating Schwann cell normally forms one internode on one axon;
- after injury, activate a repair programme, clear debris, provide trophic support and form bands of Büngner that guide regrowing axons.

### Satellite glia

- surround neuronal cell bodies in peripheral ganglia;
- express receptors and ion channels and regulate the local neuronal environment;
- are distinct from Schwann cells despite sharing a PNS location.

## Core causal maps

### Radial-glial development

Ventricular-zone progenitor
→ radial process spans ventricular-to-pial axis
→ newborn cell uses scaffold and positional cues
→ migration and lineage progression
→ organised neural tissue

### Peripheral nerve repair

Axon injury and distal degeneration
→ Schwann-cell repair reprogramming
→ myelin/debris clearance and trophic signalling
→ aligned bands of Büngner
→ regrowth toward the target
→ possible reinnervation

### Reactive astrocyte trade-off

CNS injury
→ context-dependent astrocyte-state change
→ lesion border contains inflammation and protects surviving tissue
→ scar-associated signals can restrict later axon extension
→ effective repair requires timing and state control rather than blanket ablation

## Experiment logic

### Microglial fate mapping

- **Manipulation:** Label early yolk-sac macrophage progenitors and trace descendants.
- **Result:** Labelled embryonic progenitors contribute persistently to adult microglia.
- **Inference:** Microglial ancestry differs from neuroectoderm-derived CNS macroglia.
- **Limit:** Ancestry does not determine every adult state or function.

### Astrocyte-scar perturbation

- **Manipulation:** Prevent or ablate scar-forming astrocytes after spinal cord injury and measure lesion spread and stimulated axon growth.
- **Result:** Removing scar formation does not automatically improve regrowth and can remove tissue-protective functions.
- **Inference:** Reactive borders can be both protective and growth-limiting.
- **Limit:** Broad cell ablation does not isolate one secreted factor or one reactive state.

### Schwann-cell repair programme

- **Manipulation:** Disrupt injury-induced c-Jun signalling selectively in Schwann cells.
- **Result:** Repair-cell organisation, trophic support, axon regeneration and recovery are impaired.
- **Inference:** PNS regeneration depends on active Schwann-cell reprogramming.
- **Limit:** Rodent nerve distance and recovery time do not reproduce long human nerves.

## Examiner traps

- “Support cell” is not a mechanism. Name the glial cell, action, location and consequence.
- GFAP is not an absolute all-astrocyte-and-only-astrocyte identity marker.
- Astrocytes regulate the blood–brain barrier; endothelial tight junctions form its principal physical seal.
- M1/M2 microglia and A1/A2 astrocytes do not exhaust the multidimensional states found in vivo.
- A glial scar can restrict axon growth and contain inflammatory damage. Both functions can be true.
- Preserving a neuron or axon does not establish correct target reconnection or recovered function.

## Long-answer skeleton

**Prompt:** Compare glial responses to injury in the CNS and PNS and explain why their effects cannot be classified as simply helpful or harmful.

1. Thesis: injury changes glial state; outcome depends on cell identity, time, location and measured endpoint.
2. Explain Schwann-cell repair reprogramming, clearance, trophic support and bands of Büngner.
3. Explain reactive astrocyte lesion containment and scar-associated growth restriction.
4. Add microglial surveillance, inflammatory signalling and phagocytosis.
5. Predict effects of selectively perturbing one response.
6. Separate cell survival, axon extension, target reconnection and behaviour as distinct readouts.
7. State model and translation limits.
